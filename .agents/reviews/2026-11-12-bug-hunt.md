# zujose bug hunt, 2026-11-12

Commit `7e94492`. Hunters: 3 on Fable 5.2, run one at a time, slices: the
R layer, what zujose decodes, what zujose emits and how its helpers
compose. Coordinator on Opus 5.5. Built here: the whole package (pure R),
against zucrypt 0.1.0.9000 with every algorithm of `zujose_info()`
available. Counterparts: PyJWT 2.15.1, jwcrypto 1.5.6 and cryptography 50.0.2.
Unverified: a zucrypt older than the development version (the
`zujose_unsupported_error` paths), and a non-UTF-8 multibyte locale.

Every finding was re-run by the coordinator against a fresh install of
`7e94492` (`2026-11-12-bug-hunt-probes/rerun/`). The probe scripts are
beside this file. They hard-code the hunt's scratch paths, so edit the
`.libPaths()` line before running them.

No signature forgery, algorithm confusion or decryption oracle was found.
The cryptography held against every counterpart. The findings are in how R
values become JSON, in key import, and in header composition.

Already filed and found again: #91 (by all three hunters), #94 (by two).

## 1. Refuse `zip` in every header jwe_encrypt() takes — #117, high, reproduced

- Where: `R/jwe.R:181-187`
- Trigger: `jwe_encrypt("x", kw, "A128KW", format = "flattened",
  unprotected = list(zip = "DEF"))`, or a `jwe_recipient()` header with
  `zip`. jwcrypto: "Error -3 while decompressing data"; zujose refuses its
  own token.
- Cause: the guard reads `names(header)` only.
- Fix: refuse `zip` in `header`, `unprotected` and recipient headers.
- Family: 3. Siblings checked: `alg` and `enc` share the guard (#129).
- Test the suite lacked: `zip` through each header, in each format.

## 2. Refuse repeated expectations in jwt_decode(claims = ) — #118, medium, reproduced

- Test the suite lacked: each shape refused with an argument error.

## Cross-cutting

- **The encoder trusts R's shape.** #119, #120 and #123 share one root:
  the encoders check an argument's type and length, then hand the value to
  zujson as it is. Names, repeated names, `NA` names, data frames, raw and
  complex values all reach the wire, or fail in zujson. One recursive
  "JSON-safe" check at the top of every encoder would close all three.
- **Two parsers of one thing.** Key members are read once by
  `check_*_jwk()` through zucrypt, which normalises, and kept as received
  (#94, #124, #125). `key_ops` is read by a looser rule than `use` (#121).
- **Candidate rule for `correctness.md`, a fifth family: "R attributes
  change the meaning."** A value whose `names` or class (factor, POSIXct,
  data frame) changes what a serializer writes or what `identical()`
  compares, while the type check passes. Test: every scalar argument
  given its named form must give the same result as its unnamed one.
- **Candidate rule: "no `$` on data the caller or a token supplied."**
  `$` matches partially; `[[` does not. #122 is the instance.

## Held: what was probed and stood

- **JWS:** 2,883 tokens over 12 algorithms × 3 formats × `b64` ×
  detached × 6 payloads verified by cryptography over an independently
  rebuilt signing input. ES signatures were the full fixed length,
  including about 700 per curve with a leading-zero `r` or `s`. PSS salt
  length was the digest size. PyJWT, cryptography and jwcrypto tokens of
  all 12 algorithms verified. Malformed and weak signatures were refused:
  high-S, r = 0, s + n, DER, PKCS1 and PSS swapped, MGF1-SHA1, 1024-bit
  RSA.
- **JWE:** 792 zujose tokens decrypted by jwcrypto, over every
  `alg` × `enc` × format, plaintexts of 0 to 100 bytes (block edges
  included), `aad`, unprotected and per-recipient headers. 648 more round
  trips. Concat KDF, AES key wrap, `content_encrypt` and the PBES2 KEK were
  byte-identical to cryptography in 360 fixed-input cases (Concat KDF up
  to 512 bits). 2,600 fresh ECDH ephemerals were all read by jwcrypto.
  Every jwcrypto token tested decrypted.
- **JWE oracle:** over eight algorithms and every mutation of the
  encrypted key, IV, ciphertext and tag, there was one class, one message
  and no extra fields.
- **Policy before cryptography:** an over-limit `p2c`, an off-curve
  `epk` and a disallowed `alg` were refused before the resolver ran.
  Header keys (`jwk`, `jku`) were never used.
- **Parsing:** strict Base64url, repeated members in and across headers,
  `crit` and `b64` rules, the three serializations of one token agreeing,
  and `require = "all"` and `"any"` over mixed signatures.
- **Keys:** 1,064 generated keys read by jwcrypto and cryptography. EC
  coordinates were full size, RSA integers minimal, PEM forms loaded, and
  thumbprints equal to jwcrypto's for every `kty`. `jwk_public()` leaked
  no private member, and printing showed no secret.
- **JWT:** PyJWT decoded every algorithm. The `exp`, `nbf`, `iat` and
  `max_age` boundaries held at `leeway` 0 and 60. `aud`, `iss` and `sub`
  shapes, nested and encrypted JWTs, and depth limits held.
- **R layer:** the claim value matrix (integers above 2^31 and 2^53,
  long doubles, Date, POSIXct, non-ASCII, latin1, newlines) round-tripped
  exactly. Token bytes did not change under `LC_ALL=C`, `OutDec`,
  `scipen`, `digits` or a non-UTC `TZ`, apart from #137. The closures and
  their direct forms gave identical results.

## Not reached

- Timing behaviour (RSA1_5, HMAC comparison, padding check).
- `p2c` near 2^32 on encrypt: it would take hours.
- The limits at their real defaults (1 MiB): only small values were
  straddled for the decode paths.
- A resolver whose keys change between signatures or recipients.
- A systematic audit of every message for echoed key or plaintext bytes.
  None was seen.

## Not reproduced, and by design

- **A single `jwk` ignores the token's `kid`.** By design (`R/select.R`
  header comment). Every other finding reproduced.
- **Unprotected header members are not counted by `limits$header`.** By
  design: `?jose_limits` says "protected header".
